## Main proposed refinement

The original manuscript establishes a bound of the form

```math
W_r(k)>k^{c k\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}
```

with $c=1/100000$, for sufficiently large $k$ and every integer $r\ge2$. Our conditional parameter-transfer argument admits **$c=1/9$**, with the threshold independent of $r$, and every fixed $c\lt1/8$ in the short-period region below.

That is a **$100000/9\approx11{,}111$-fold increase in the coefficient inside the exponent** (and $25/18\approx1.39$ times the previous explicit choice $2/25$). The earlier $2/25$, $1/16$, $1/25$, $1/193$, and $1/200$ choices remain included for comparison. No explicit finite coloring record or runtime improvement follows. The threshold may be very large and is not made explicit here.

The earlier [repeated-key certificate cover](#repeated-key-certificate-cover-conditional-119) admits $c=1/19$, a factor $100000/19\approx5263$ over the source.

### Why the argument changes

The source bounds the multiplicity of a geometric key by four. Its affinity and separation properties place points of one key on an affine real line, at mutual distances at least one, with squared norms in a half-open interval $[m,m+1)$.

In fact there are at most **two** such points. Parameterize the line by signed arclength $t$ from its nearest point to the origin, so squared norm is $t^2+C$. Among three parameters, two lie on the same half-line. Their absolute values satisfy $v-u\ge1$, hence

```math
v^2-u^2=(v-u)(v+u)\ge1,
```

contradicting membership in a common half-open interval of width one. This elementary lemma is proved directly; applying it to the original construction still requires the source's hypotheses.

Using this multiplicity bound, write $D=\lceil k^a\rceil$, $M=\lceil k^m\rceil$, and $p=k^{-b}$. The note derives the sufficient region

```math
a,b,c>0,\quad 3a\lt m\lt 1,\quad b\lt a,\quad 3a\lt 1-b,
```

```math
0<\delta<1,\quad 0<\gamma\le\delta/4,\quad \gamma<(1-\delta)/8,\quad 2c<\gamma b/2.
```

| Choice | $a$ | $m$ | $b$ | $\delta$ | $\gamma$ | $c$ | Coefficient ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original dimension scales | 1/10 | 1/2 | 1/20 | 1/10 | 1/40 | 1/4000 | 25 |
| Retuned parameters | 1/5 | 3/4 | 1/6 | 1/10 | 1/40 | 1/1000 | 100 |
| Earlier 500-fold choice | 1/4 | 4/5 | 247/1000 | 1/3 | 41/500 | 1/200 | 500 |
| New proposed choice | 1/4 | 4/5 | 2499/10000 | 1/3 | 83/1000 | 1/193 | 100000/193 |

The new choice has strictly positive exact margins and stays below the retained region's endpoint $1/192$. Its full transfer argument and margin table are in [near-limit-proof.md](near-limit-proof.md).

For the earlier 500-fold row, $\gamma b/2-2c=127/1000000$ and both affine entropy power gaps equal $3/1000$. These strict margins are checked with exact rational arithmetic in [parameters.json](parameters.json) and [audit_math.py](audit_math.py).

The same sufficient region admits every fixed positive $c<1/192$. The endpoint is not asserted, and this is only a supremum within the retained estimates. The full transfer proof and its dependencies are in the note; passing the scripts does not establish that transfer as a theorem.

### Breaking the $1/192$ ceiling: the sharpened region

The $1/192$ supremum comes from two fixed choices inside the source argument, not from its geometry. First, the outer coloring is only asked to place **one quarter** of each bit on every tested set of labels, which caps $\gamma$ at $1/12$. Second, the short-period cutoff $h_0=D^2$ makes the global label entropy $k^{3a}$, which caps $b$ at $1/4$. The note's new Section 4 changes three things, with the source's geometric lemmas, anchored pattern count, affinity lemma, prime bound, and digit product untouched:

1. **Shorter dilation.** Take $h_0=D\lceil\log k\rceil^2$ and $\eta=\varepsilon_{\rm r}/(8D)$. The dilation lemma still holds, the nonregular fraction becomes $\le\varepsilon_{\rm r}$, and the global entropy drops to $O(k^{2a}\log^3k)$.
2. **Sharper balance.** Ask the local lemma for a $(1/2-\varepsilon)$ fraction of each bit instead of one quarter. The Hoeffding-type tail $2e^{-2\varepsilon^2 l}$ and weights $e^{-\varepsilon^2 l}$ satisfy the asymmetric local-lemma criterion for every fixed $\varepsilon$, since all tested sizes are unbounded.
3. **Balance the light positions outside heavy blocks.** Those positions are a function of the label word, so they get their own first-family event. Heavy blocks and these light positions then cover all but $h+\delta k=o(k)+\delta k$ positions.

The resulting sufficient region is

```math
a,b,c>0,\quad 2a\lt m\lt 1,\quad b\lt a,\quad 2a\lt 1-b,
```

```math
0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12,\quad 0<\varepsilon_{\rm r}\le\tfrac12,\quad 0<\delta,\quad
\gamma<(\tfrac12-\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon_{\rm r})(1-\delta),\quad 2c<\gamma b/2,
```

so $b\lt1/3$, $\gamma\lt1/2$, and $c\lt\gamma b/4\lt1/24$; every fixed $c\lt1/24$ is admitted.

| Choice | $a$ | $m$ | $b$ | $\delta$ | $\varepsilon$ | $\varepsilon_{\rm r}$ | $\gamma$ | $c$ | Coefficient ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sharpened explicit choice | 333/1000 | 4/5 | 331/1000 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 97/200 | 1/25 | 4000 |

Its decisive margins are $\gamma b/2-2c=107/400000$, $1-b-2a=3/1000$, $a-b=1/500$, and $(1/2-\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon_{\rm r})(1-\delta)-\gamma=40499/8000000$. The full argument and margin table are in [sharpened-region-proof.md](sharpened-region-proof.md); `audit_math.py` checks them exactly and rejects five boundary controls, including $c=1/24$ itself. Those controls test the earlier condition $2c\lt\gamma b/2$; the new certificate-cover region below is not yet checked by `audit_math.py`.

## Repeated-key certificate cover: conditional 1/19


### Why $1/24$ is a ceiling of this architecture

Every argument here, and the source manuscript, follows one template: a label-based outer coloring that balances every non-affine progression, keys with at most $\mu$ occurrences per progression carrying Bernoulli($k^{-b}$) flips, a union bound over the $N^2$ progressions for the balanced case, and a union bound over affine signatures for the affine case. [barrier-note.md](barrier-note.md) proves that within this template with $\mu=2$, affine key-word entropy $(6+o(1))\log q$, and label entropy $k^{2a+o(1)}$, every admissible coefficient satisfies $c\lt1/24$: the rich bound gives $c\lt\gamma b/4$, balance gives $\gamma\le1/2$, and the affine bound forces $b\lt a$ and $2a+b\le1$, so $3b\lt2a+b\le1$ and $b\lt1/3$.

The note then records, feature by feature, why the three hypotheses resist change: the two-point multiplicity is the reflection symmetry of any quadratic band along a line, and distinguishing the pair costs about $D$ functionals worth $2\log q$ each; the $6\log q$ is tight for floor-words of quadratics; the $k^{2a}$ comes from $2D\log\lambda$ with $\log\lambda\gtrsim h_0\gtrsim D$ forced by the nonregular fraction; and a local-lemma pass over the flips reproduces the $N^2$ union bound because a key's fiber has about $Nk^{-2D}$ elements. AND-ing two flip layers, modular or widened bands, deterministic band parities, and coloring bands in the outer layer are each shown to fail. These explanations are not theorems. `audit_math.py` confirms the ceiling $1/24$ exactly (`template_ceiling`). Going further needs a different construction, not different parameters.

### Past $1/24$: a rank-split cover of rich events

The sharpened region's last binding inequality, $2c\lt\gamma b/2$, comes from a union bound over all $q^{2D}$ progressions in which each repeated key contributes only one flip. The merged repeated-key proposal, [rohanarun/van-der-waerden-refinement#4](https://github.com/rohanarun/van-der-waerden-refinement/pull/4), observed that repeated keys can be certified with entropy about $q^D$. That gives $c\lt\gamma b/3$ and every $c\lt1/18$. [rank-certificate-proof.md](rank-certificate-proof.md) restates its repeated-key facts with credit, then splits rich events by the **carry rank** $\rho$ of the residue classes that contain repeated keys:

1. **Start-free certificate.** Every key in those classes, singles included, is fixed by $3+\rho$ real projections. These key sets therefore number $q^{2\rho+o(D)}$, and also $q^{D+o(D)}$ by the anchored count.
2. **Rank count.** Each such class pins an integer linear form in the base-$q$ digits of the step. Progressions of rank $\rho$ therefore number at most $q^{2D-\rho+o(D)}$.

Rich events are then split by whether at least $\theta k$ flipped keys lie in those classes. The worst rank is $\rho=D/2$, and the rich condition becomes

```math
5c\lt2\gamma b,
```

with every other inequality of the sharpened region unchanged. Hence every fixed $c\lt2\gamma b/5\lt1/15$ is admitted.

| Choice | $a$ | $m$ | $b$ | $\delta$ | $\varepsilon$ | $\varepsilon_{\rm r}$ | $\gamma$ | $c$ | Coefficient ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rank-split explicit choice | 333/1000 | 4/5 | 331/1000 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 97/200 | 1/16 | 6250 |

The exponents and balance constants are those of the $1/25$ choice; only the coefficient changes. The decisive margin is $2\gamma b/5-c=857/500000$. `audit_math.py` checks:

- every margin, in exact fractions;
- the worst rank $\rho=D/2$, where the two costs sum to $5/2$ (in units of $ck\log k$);
- the two group margins on a grid of ranks;
- the new identities, symbolically;
- the digit pinning, on 2,000 exact instances.

It also rejects five boundary controls, including $c=1/15$. The value $1/16$ lies outside both earlier rich conditions, so it is not a reparametrization of them.

### Repeated keys cost nothing: the digit rank

The rank-split argument paid $2\log q$ per direction of the carry span $V$ in its certificate, but saved only $\log q$ per direction in its progression count. [digit-rank-proof.md](digit-rank-proof.md) observes that both sides are made of base-$q$ digits in the same pattern:

- **Start side.** $q^2\langle Y_{a_0},v\rangle$ is fixed, up to a bounded tail, by two integer forms in the start point's digits.
- **Step side.** Each repeated pair fixes the same two forms in the step's digits: the first exactly, the second up to nine values.

Both counts are therefore governed by one **digit rank** $R=\dim(S_1V+S_2V)$. The certificate costs $R\log q$ and the progression count $(2D-R)\log q$, so their sum is $2D$ for **every** $R$. The rich condition becomes

```math
2c\lt\gamma b,
```

which is what the union bound gives when no key repeats at all. Every other inequality of the sharpened region is unchanged, so every fixed $c\lt\gamma b/2\lt1/12$ is admitted.

| Choice | $a$ | $m$ | $b$ | $\delta$ | $\varepsilon$ | $\varepsilon_{\rm r}$ | $\gamma$ | $c$ | Coefficient ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digit-rank explicit choice | 333/1000 | 4/5 | 331/1000 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 97/200 | 2/25 | 8000 |

These are again the exponents and balance constants of the $1/25$ choice. The decisive margin is $\gamma b/2-c=107/400000$, the same margin that certified $1/25$ under $2c\lt\gamma b/2$; the coefficient doubles. `audit_math.py` checks:

- every margin, in exact fractions;
- $\kappa+\nu=2$ for every digit rank;
- both group margins on a grid of ranks;
- the digit decompositions, on 3,000 exact instances;
- $\rho\le R\le\min(2\rho,D)$, on random carry spans.

It also rejects five boundary controls, including $c=1/12$. On the rich side, $2c\lt\gamma b$ is also what progressions with no repeated key give on their own. Going past $1/12$ would therefore need $\gamma$ above $1/2$, $b$ above $1/3$, or a new idea for those progressions.

### Past $1/12$: short-period runs

The digit-rank region still carries the sharpened region's affine inequality $2a\lt1-b$, which caps $b$ at $1/3$. It comes from paying for the full label word, $\log T_{\rm glob}=O(k^{2a}\log^3k)$, in the estimate for progressions that are not $\gamma$-rich. [short-period-runs-proof.md](short-period-runs-proof.md) observes that those progressions are short-period, so their color word is much cheaper to pin down:

1. **Runs.** In a short-period progression, the occurrences of each full label form one run: one residue class modulo the period, consecutive within it.
2. **A third local-lemma family.** Only $e^{O(D\log k)}$ short-period label words pass through a given label, so the local lemma can also balance their light positions (multiplicity at most $k/M_s$, with $M_s=D\lceil\log k\rceil^2$).
3. **Two cases.** If light positions fill at least $\delta_sk$ of the progression, about half of them need a flip, and the event is covered by the key word alone. Otherwise at most $M_s$ heavy runs fix the color word, at a cost of $e^{O(D\log^3k)}$.

No full label word is counted, so $2a\lt1-b$ becomes $a+b\lt1$, which already follows from $b\lt a\lt1/2$. The binding constraints are now $2a\lt m\lt1$ and $b\lt a$, so $b\lt1/2$. With the rich condition $2c\lt\gamma b$, every fixed $c\lt\gamma b/2\lt1/8$ is admitted.

| Choice | $a$ | $m$ | $b$ | $\delta$ | $\varepsilon$ | $\varepsilon_{\rm r}$ | $\gamma$ | $c$ | Coefficient ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short-period explicit choice | 49/100 | 99/100 | 97/200 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 1/200 | 97/200 | 1/9 | 100000/9 |

The light-run threshold is $\delta_s=1/2$. The decisive margin is $\gamma b/2-c=4681/720000$, and $m-2a$, $1-m$, $a-b$, $1-a-b$ are $1/100$, $1/100$, $1/200$, $1/40$. The old affine gap $1-b-2a=-93/200$ fails on purpose, so this choice lies outside the digit-rank region. `audit_math.py` checks:

- every margin, in exact fractions;
- that the old affine gap fails;
- the supremum $b\lt1/2$, by linear programming.

It also rejects five boundary controls, including $c=1/8$.

## Companion result and unsuccessful searches

For the selected scalar matrix-profile constraints, let $u=\min(a,b)$ and $v=\max(a,b)$. The explicit profile

```math
P_*(a,b)=\left(\frac{3u-1}{2}\right)^{1/3}\left(v+\frac{u-1}{2}\right)
```

is symmetric, positive, nondecreasing, and separately concave, with $P_*(1,b)=b$. It satisfies shifted tripling and the rank envelope at $t=3/4$, while its diagonal grows as $\Theta(a^{4/3})$. Thus those listed constraints alone cannot exclude $t=3/4$. **This is not an actual tensor character or a lower bound on the matrix-multiplication exponent.** The note gives an analytic proof; finite LP experiments are exploratory.

The Sidorenko search found **no smaller certificate**: 56,984 labeled candidates in the contraction/flip run, and 28,281 in the weighted-cut run, of which 2,518 passed the discrete and degree screen. These nonexhaustive searches may overlap and include isomorphic duplicates. Floating-point LP failures are not infeasibility certificates. The original 35-vertex, 66-edge finite certificate passes the independent combinatorial checker; this does not verify the full analytic counterexample proof.

No improved unit-distance exponent was obtained.

## What still needs review

The main outstanding work is independent verification of the complete van der Waerden parameter transfer, including uniform constants and all imported hypotheses. The sharpened region additionally relies on the three modifications in Section 4 of the note (shorter dilation, $(1/2-\varepsilon)$-balance in the local lemma, and the separate balancing of light positions outside heavy blocks); each is argued in [sharpened-region-proof.md](sharpened-region-proof.md) but has not been independently reviewed by a human. The rank-split region further relies on Lemmas A–C of [rank-certificate-proof.md](rank-certificate-proof.md), and on the repeated-key structure of the merged proposal #4, which is restated there. Neither has been independently reviewed by a human. The digit-rank region replaces those lemmas with Lemmas A\* and C\* of [digit-rank-proof.md](digit-rank-proof.md), also not independently reviewed by a human. The short-period region further relies on Lemmas 1 and 2 and the third local-lemma family of [short-period-runs-proof.md](short-period-runs-proof.md), likewise not independently reviewed by a human. Pushing past $1/8$ would need $\gamma$ above $1/2$, $b$ above $1/2$ (which $b\lt a$ and $2a\lt m\lt1$ forbid for these keys and this outer coloring), or a new idea for rich progressions without repeated keys. Literature priority has not been established. For matrix multiplication, progress requires additional valid tensor constraints or a different construction. Any smaller Sidorenko finite object would still need the full analytic transfer before it could be called a new counterexample.

The [barrier note](barrier-note.md) applies to the earlier full-progression rich-event template. The later certificate and short-period arguments change that template and are consistent with its stated barrier.
